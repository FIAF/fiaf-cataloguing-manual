    8.3 Elements of a Manifestation

    8.3.1 Identifier¶

    Create an unambiguous reference to the Manifestation, such as a specific standard number issued by an official body (i.e. V-ISAN4 or DOI5), or a system-automated assigned ID number. These should be different identifiers than those for Works/Variants, since they are for specific embodiments of the Work/Variant.

    If one or more identifiers are available, record each according to its standardised syntax, where allowable. The following examples reflect both 3 and 4 level hierarchies, i.e. ones that use Variants and ones that do not have Variants but utilise Manifestations instead.

    Example

    Lola rennt (Germany, 1998, Tom Tykwer)

    Work identifier: ISAN 0000-0000-606A-0000-0-0000-0000-3

    Variant identifier (original German): ISAN 0000-0000-606A-0000-0-0000-0002-0

    Manifestation identifier: Home video publication (2007) – Run Lola Run – DVD – English – V-ISAN: ISAN 0000-0000-606A-0000-0-0000-0001-1 (DVD-31943)

    Example

    Shichinin no samurai (Japan, 1954, Akira Kurosawa)

    Work identifier: ISAN 0000-0001-61AE-0000-1-0000-0001-W

    Variant identifier (Dubbed Spanish – Castellano): Los Siete Samurai: ISAN 0000-0001-61AE-0000-1-0000-0001-W

    Example

    Les chevaliers du ciel (France, 2005, Gérard Pirès)

    Manifestation identifier: Home video publication – DVD – Fox Pathé Europa (publisher) – 2006 – ISAN 0000-0001-8CFA-0000-I-0000-000-

    Example

    8 1/2 (Italy, 1962, Federico Fellini) Otto e mezzo – Alternative title of the Work

    Work identifier: ISAN 0000-0000-161F-0000-W-0000-0000-F

    Manifestation identifier: V- ISAN 0000-0000-161F-0000-W-0000-0002-B – Italian (spoken); Japanese (subtitles) – DVD – Blu-ray – 2013/01/11 – Japan)

    8.3.1.1 Identifier Type¶

    If an institution’s system allows, as with the Work/Variant Identifiers a “Type” can be applied with an Identifier to define the source of the Identifier.

    8.3.2 Title¶

    Record at least one title, identifying phrase, or name for the Manifestation Title. This is a transcribed element, reflecting the actual title as it appears on screen. The original release title would be added under the Work/Variant Title field.

    If multiple titles are recorded, where allowable, associate a “Title Type” to a title for differentiation between the various Title Types).

    The title of a Manifestation can sometimes differ, either slightly or wholly from the title of the Variant or Work to which it is linked. This may be the case, for example, with the acquisition of an incomplete Manifestation lacking a title or with a title added by the source of the acquisition.

    See Title for further titling details and information.

    For creating titles for untitled or unidentified entities see Supplied/Devised Titles (i.e. Creating titles for untitled/unidentified entities or production material)

    See Definition for titling of Aggregates.

    For guidance on wording, order, spelling, punctuation, accentuation and capitalisation, see Purpose.

    For sources of information for the Manifestation Title, see Sources of Information.

    8.3.2.1 Title Type¶

    Where relevant for an institution, if the Manifestation includes multiple titles, such as a title in another language for a dubbed or subtitled variant, record the relevant titles and associate a “Title Type” to each title for differentiation between the various types of titles (see Titles and Title Types).

    8.3.3 Language¶

    “Language” should ideally be presented as a set of two elements: the language term, and its usage in a particular Manifestation. Language can be present in a Manifestation as written, spoken or sung. Its usage qualifies whether the specific language is dialogue, dubbed, subtitles, intertitles, etc.

    Language and usage information can help distinguish language versions described as separate Variants or Manifestations (see Boundaries between Manifestations and Works or Variants and Changes in the Publication Context). However, selectable soundtracks or subtitles within one digital object do not, by themselves, require separate Variants or Manifestations.

    8.3.3.1 Language Terms¶

    Where possible, record the language(s) using the full form of name, e.g., French, Italian, etc. rather than abbreviations or codes, by taking the most suitable value(s) from a controlled list.

    This can be an in-house list but it is preferable to use a standard language list such as ISO 639 (http://www.loc.gov/standards/iso639-2/php/code_list.php).

    If no language can be determined, the information can be omitted or indicated by a value of “not known”.

    8.3.3.2 Usage Type¶

    More than one language can occur in different forms, depending on how the content is expressed: the usage type of the languages defines the form with which the language is expressed, for example, spoken, sung, written, etc.

    Record the usage type of a language by taking the most suitable value from a controlled list. A suggested list, which is open and not exhaustive, can be found in Language Usage Types.

    If usage type(s) cannot be determined, indicate a value of “unknown”.

    Example

    Caccia tragica (Italy, Giuseppe De Santis, 1947)

    Not for release (archival) – 35mm – Italian (spoken), Romanian (intertitles)

    Example

    New York stories, (United States of America, Woody Allen, Francis Ford Coppola, Martin Scorsese, 1989)

    Home video publication – DVD - English (spoken); Italian, German, French, Spanish (subtitles) – Buena Vista (publisher), 2002/05/16

    Example

    The Bridge. Episode 1 (Sweden, Denmark, Charlotte Sieling, 2011)

    TV broadcast – United Kingdom, 2012-04-21 - Swedish (Dialogue (original)), Danish (Dialogue (original)), English (Subtitles)

    8.3.3.3 Multiple language options for Digital Manifestations/Items¶

    Digital objects may offer multiple selectable soundtracks or subtitle options within a single file or across related files. Examples include DVD and Blu-ray publications and objects acquired from streaming services.

    There are different options for capturing this, depending on the systems used, materials acquired, and decisions made by your institution on the extent of data to be captured.

    There are largely three main possible approaches:

    1. Core description: record only the principal or relevant language(s) necessary for identification and access.
    2. Extended Manifestation/Item description: distinguish languages presented together from selectable alternative soundtracks or subtitles.
    3. Full descriptive approach: record the complete language configuration when this is significant for an institution's collection, users, preservation responsibilities or access requirements.

    Where language options are recorded in detail, distinguish languages presented together from alternative soundtracks or subtitles that the user can select, for example through fields for Language and Usage alongside fields for Selectable Language and Usage. Where relevant, identify the original-language soundtrack and dubbed alternatives. Make clear which language options are available, without implying that all are presented simultaneously. Where such fields are not available, this information may be recorded using other descriptive elements or notes.

    Example

    New York stories, (United States of America, Woody Allen, Francis Ford Coppola, Martin Scorsese, 1989)

    Home video publication – DVD, English (spoken); – Selectable language alternatives Italian, German (dubbed); English, Italian, German, French, Spanish, (subtitles) – Buena Vista (publisher), 2002/05/16

    The same Language field could be used for both, but this risks ambiguity about which languages are presented together and which are selectable alternatives, and could imply that all the languages indicated appear at the same time. If a single field is used, add a qualifying note for clarity. Alternatively, an additional field alongside Language and Usage, indicating whether each language is presented or selectable, would aid clarity.

    It is not necessary to create a separate Variant or Manifestation for each selectable language option. Depending on the cataloguing system, representing each option separately may lead to duplicate Item records for the same object. This should not give the impression that several Items exist when the options belong to a single Item.

    Where the cataloguing system allows one Item to be associated with more than one Manifestation, an institution may describe selectable language options through separate Manifestations linked to that Item, for example:

    An English film Work is linked to three Home Viewing Manifestations of a Blu-ray release: original English, German subtitles, and French subtitles. All three Manifestations link to one Blu-ray Item in English, with German or French subtitles available for the viewer to select.

    Where Variants are used and the cataloguing system allows one Manifestation to be associated with more than one Variant, an institution may describe language options through separate Variants linked to that Manifestation, with the Item linked to the same Manifestation, for example:

    An English film Work is linked to three Variants/Expressions: original English, a German-subtitled version, and a French-subtitled version. All three link to one Home Viewing Manifestation of a Blu-ray release. That Manifestation links to one Blu-ray Item in English, with German or French subtitles available for the viewer to select.

    This structure is modelled on an RDA-based approach and may be useful for institutions following RDA. Note that this manual's Variants are not equivalent to RDA Expressions (see Definitions of the Work and Variant Entities).

    Where separate Variants or Manifestations describe selectable language options, make clear that those options are available within one Item. They should not be mistaken for an aggregate containing several complete versions of the moving image presented one after another.

    Where selectable-language information is recorded, it is important to make clear which options are actually available in the Item, including any differences from the Manifestation description.

    A recording of a streamed moving image may contain only the selected soundtrack and subtitles, not all the language options offered for that moving image. Similarly, a digital object acquired directly from a streaming provider may include only the language options specified in the delivery agreement.

    It is also possible that language(s) are added as part of digitalisation, preservation or other technological processes so it will be important to be able to specify which languages were added in relation to particular Items in those instances.

    The Item should record the actual characteristics of the material held by an institution rather than simply inherit all the possibilities of the Manifestation.

    Alternatively, an institution may take the “core description” approach and choose not to record every selectable language option, but prioritise the language(s) most relevant to its users’ needs and its preservation and access responsibilities.

    8.3.4 Physical and Digital Description of a Manifestation¶

    As previously mentioned, a Manifestation is defined on the basis of two criteria: changes in the publication context and changes in format. Physical and digital description records technical characteristics such as format, projection, sound, and colour. For a Manifestation describing a particular release or edition, its expected characteristics can provide a reference for the associated Items, regardless of which Items are held in a collection.

    This reference does not imply that every Item associated with a Manifestation has the same technical characteristics. As explained in Moving Image Manifestations, a Pre-Release Manifestation may group different production materials, and institutions may associate in-house derivatives with the same Manifestation as their sources. A single “ideal” technical format need not apply to every Item in such a grouping.

    Manifestation-level characteristics may be reused in Item descriptions where they apply to the object being described. Item descriptions should record the actual characteristics of each object; characteristics should not be assumed to apply solely because the Item is linked to that Manifestation. In some databases, selection of a format or broad format category initiates provision of element fields relevant to that format at a Manifestation level, an Item level, or both, for example in a 2-Level hierarchy. See “Shallow hierarchy model: 2 levels” found at Elements of description across Works, Variants, Manifestations, and Items.

    Ideally the information need only be recorded once irrespective of where in the data structure an institution must place it. Therefore guidelines for the treatment of high-level physical and digital description elements are explained fully in the Manifestation chapter. The Item chapter contains a detailed listing of item-specific elements.

    Where Extent and Format at Manifestation level describe an expected or “ideal” release, Item-specific information records the actual material and any differences from that reference. Where the Manifestation groups varied materials, describe each Item on its own terms rather than treating its different characteristics as deviations from a single ideal object.

    Differences in format can help distinguish Manifestations. Apply the criteria and institutional options described in Boundaries between Manifestations, including the option of associating an in-house copy in a different format with the same Manifestation as its source.

    In a note (See Cataloguer’s Notes), explain the format changes used to determine the Manifestation in hand as different and “new” in comparison with any other, already identified and described Manifestation.

    The physical and digital description of a Manifestation includes the following elements:

    • Format
    • Projection characteristics
    • Sound characteristics
    • Colour characteristics

    8.3.4.1 Format¶

    Format describes the physical or digital technical form in which content is fixed, including the physical medium, recording format, digital file format, or package format. It helps identify the technology, equipment, or software required to read, decode, reproduce, or play back that content. Other technical characteristics may be needed to establish the full playback requirements.

    This develops the information addressed through Carrier type and Gauge within EN 15907's broader Format description.18 Other technical characteristics, including aspect ratio, sound, colour, and digital codecs, are described separately in this manual.

    Cataloguing systems may record format information in one field or distribute it across several fields, such as General Carrier Type and Specific Carrier Type, as used in the first edition of this manual (2016).19 Use the available structure consistently so that the technical format of the object can be identified. For digital materials, distinguish the format of the digital object from that of the physical storage media holding its copies.

    Use terminology appropriate to the collection and cataloguing system, drawing on a controlled vocabulary where available. Broad designations such as Film, Audio, Video, or Digital file may support initial identification and searching. Record more specific technical format information where known and relevant. This guidance does not require separate general and specific fields or a hierarchical vocabulary.

    For film and tape, distinguish gauge—the width of the material—from format. A format designation may include its gauge, but different formats may use material of the same width. For example, Standard 8 and Super 8 share the same nominal gauge but differ in perforation and image dimensions. Gauge remains a distinct descriptive attribute and may be recorded separately, even when it is also included in the format designation.

    For digital objects, distinguish container formats, image file formats, and package formats. A package may contain files in several formats; an image sequence may comprise many files in one image format. Describe the component formats where needed to make their application clear. Codecs describe the encoding of the digital content and should not be treated as interchangeable with container or package formats.

    Authored audio and video disc formats should be distinguished from optical media used to store digital objects. Whether a disc is writable or commercially produced does not, by itself, identify the technical format of its contents. Where physical storage media are also recorded in the catalogue, keep their characteristics distinguishable from those of the digital objects. See Moving Image Items for the scope of this distinction.

    The following terms are examples, not exhaustive lists or a prescribed classification. They are grouped by the kind of equipment or software needed, as described above. Audio and video carrier formats may record analogue or digital signals; file-based audio and video objects are described through their digital formats.

    Format Examples
    Film Standard 8mm film, Super 8mm film, 9.5mm film, 16mm film, 35mm film, 35mm magnetic film, 70mm film
    Audio tapes and discs Open-reel audiotape, Audiocassette, CD (audio), DAT
    Video tapes and discs 2-inch Quadruplex, 1-inch Type C, U-matic, Betacam SP, D1, Digital Betacam, LaserDisc, DVD-Video, Blu-ray
    Digital files and packages Container formats: MOV, MXF, MKV, MP4, WAV; image file formats: DPX, TIFF, OpenEXR; package formats: DCP, DCDM, IMF
    Physical storage media LTO tape (e.g. LTO-5, LTO-6), hard disk drive (HDD), data disc such as DVD-R

    Physical storage media are listed for institutions that catalogue them. Their format describes the medium, not the digital objects it holds, which are described by their own formats.

    8.3.4.1.1 Sources for Format Terminology¶

    Institutions may develop their own controlled lists or refer to existing sources. These include SMPTE standards, engineering guidelines, and recommended practices, as well as the following:

    Examples of sources

    YEE (http://myee.bol.ucla.edu/catrul.doc) 5.3.3 (physical carriers)

    AMIM2 5D, pp. 18-19 (for gauges/width values)

    AMIM2 5B7, pp. 10-11 (including both “tape” and “disc” based video formats).

    RDA 3.20; YEE (http://myee.bol.ucla.edu/catrul.doc) 5.3.14 (for encoding formats).

    PBCore instantiationPhysical http://metadataregistry.org/concept/list/vocabulary_id/145.html (for physical carriers)

    PBCore instantiationDigital (http://pbcore.org/pbcoreinstantiation/instantiationdigital/) (for broad digital formats)

    8.3.4.2 Projection Characteristics of a Manifestation¶

    The projection characteristics of a Manifestation include aspect ratio and aperture or image format.

    Aspect Ratio/Image Ratio

    Aspect ratio (also known as the image ratio or projection ratio) expresses the relationship between image width and height. Film aspect ratios are conventionally expressed with the height normalised to 1, for example 1.37:1 or 2.39:1. Video aspect ratios are also commonly expressed as whole-number proportions, such as 4:3 or 16:9. State whether the value describes the recorded image area, the active image area within an encoded frame, or the intended presentation, where this distinction is relevant. Selection should be made from a controlled list of values. A suggested list of examples, which is open and not exhaustive, includes:

    • 1.33:1
    • 1.37:1
    • 1.19:1
    • 2.55:1
    • 2.35:1
    • 1.66:1
    • 1.75:1
    • 1.85:1
    • 2.39:1
    • 4:3

    Fuller details and examples of “aspect ratio” value lists can be found in the Technical Glossary of Common Audiovisual Terms (National Film and Sound Archive Australia), The Advanced Projection Manual and the PBCore essenceTrackAspect Ratio http://metadataregistry.org/concept/list/vocabulary_id/129.html

    The intended presentation aspect ratio reflects the compositional intentions of the original content makers.6 Distinguish alterations to the image, such as cropping or stretching, from changes to the surrounding frame, such as letterboxing or pillarboxing. Cropping may remove image information.7 Adding borders does not, by itself, change the image's proportions or remove content.

    Where applicable, describe the intended presentation at Manifestation level and the actual characteristics of the particular Item in its description.

    Institutions may record variations in projection characteristics as Item-specifics, rather than create multiple Manifestations.

    Aperture/Image Format

    For film, describe the recorded image area and the applicable aperture or image format, for example Academy or full aperture.8 Distinguish the recorded image area from the area intended to be shown in projection, which may be masked. The image format (aperture) does not necessarily correspond to the intended image ratio (projection ratio or aspect ratio) of the moving image.

    Select terms from one or more controlled vocabularies, as appropriate to the aspects being described. The following examples are illustrative, not exhaustive, and may be used in combination.

    Aperture and recording geometry

    • Academy
    • Full aperture
    • Movietone

    Named image systems

    • CinemaScope
    • Todd-AO

    Image characteristics

    • Anamorphic

    Broad presentation designations

    • Widescreen
    • Flat
    • Full Height
    • Full Screen

    Image placement and reframing

    • Pan and scan (reframing the image)
    • Pillarbox (bars added at the sides)
    • Letterbox (bars added at the top and bottom)
    • Windowbox (bars added at the sides and top and bottom)

    Stereoscopic presentation

    • 3D

    Further examples can be found in The Advanced Projection Manual.

    8.3.4.3 Sound Characteristics of a Manifestation¶

    Sound characteristics describe the presence or absence of recorded sound and, where relevant, its technical characteristics.9 The example lists below distinguish these aspects; they are suggested values, not exhaustive lists. Select terms from one or more controlled vocabularies appropriate to the aspects being described.

    Presence or absence of sound

    Select terms from a controlled vocabulary, for example:

    • Sound
    • Silent
    • Mute

    Use Silent where the moving image was made without a recorded soundtrack, as with films of the silent era.

    If the associated Work/Variant originally had sound but the Manifestation lacks it, describe it as Mute, not Silent, and explain this in a note.10

    If the associated Work/Variant was originally silent but the Manifestation includes added recorded sound, describe it as Sound and explain the addition in a note.

    Optionally, in systems that cannot record the values above, record a simple yes/no value indicating whether recorded sound is present. As this does not distinguish Silent from Mute, explain the absence of sound in a note where it is known.

    8.3.4.3.1 Sound System¶

    Describe the sound system used, using a broad category or a specific system as appropriate. Select terms from a controlled vocabulary, for example:

    • Needle sound
    • Analogue optical
    • Magnetic
    • Combined magnetic sound
    • Dolby Stereo SR
    • Dolby Digital
    • VA RCA Duplex

    Some digital sound systems, such as Dolby Digital and SDDS, are also printed optically on film. Where a broad category is used, make clear whether optical sound is analogue or digital.

    8.3.4.3.2 Noise Reduction¶

    Some sound systems, such as Dolby Stereo, incorporate noise reduction; it may also be used with other recording methods, such as magnetic sound. Where relevant, describe noise reduction separately from the sound system, for example:

    • Dolby A
    • Dolby SR

    8.3.4.3.3 Sound Channel Configuration¶

    If recorded sound is present, describe its channel configuration. Select terms from a controlled vocabulary, for example:

    • Mono
    • Stereo

    Channel configuration and sound system describe distinct but related characteristics. Some sound systems specify a particular channel arrangement, while others support several configurations. For example, 35mm optical Dolby Stereo carries left, centre, right, and surround channels matrix-encoded into two recorded channels. Distinguish the recorded arrangement from the intended reproduced configuration where relevant, and identify the configuration applicable to the soundtrack being described. The groupings above do not require separate database fields.

    8.3.4.4 Colour Characteristics of a Manifestation¶

    Distinguish the colour appearance of the image from the technical means used to produce or encode it. Colour appearance describes what is visible, including black and white and colour.11 The technical description identifies the colour system, process, or digital encoding used. A particular appearance does not, by itself, establish how it was produced.

    Colour appearance

    Record the colour characteristics of the image represented by the Manifestation. Select terms from a controlled vocabulary, for example:

    • Black and white
    • Colour
    • Black and white with applied colour

    Where an image combines different colour characteristics, record each applicable appearance term. For example, a documentary combining black-and-white archive footage with colour interviews would be described using Black and white and Colour. Describe significant variations between sections where needed.

    Some catalogues permit only one colour value. In these systems, a combined term such as Colour and black and white may represent the same characteristics.

    Black and white with applied colour describes a black-and-white photographic image coloured by tinting, toning, stencil colouring, hand colouring, or similar means. The term describes the appearance of the image, and applies equally to a photographic or digital copy that reproduces it. Record the process itself under Colour systems and processes below, in the description of the material that underwent it. A tinted nitrate print, for example, is Black and white with applied colour, with the process Tinted; a digital copy of that print has the same appearance, but Tinted is not recorded as its process.

    Where only one colour field is available, use it to describe colour appearance and record relevant process information in a note. Where an existing vocabulary uses terms such as Black and white (tinted), clarify whether the qualifier describes a treatment applied to the material being described or an appearance reproduced from a source.

    Colour systems and processes

    Where relevant, and where the catalogue records them, also record the colour system or process used in the material described. Select terms from a controlled vocabulary, for example:

    Applied colour

    • Tinted
    • Toned
    • Tinted and toned (both present in the same image)
    • Stencil colouring, for example Pathécolor
    • Hand colouring

    More specific terms may identify particular tint colours or toning processes, such as blue tinting or sepia toning. These can support searching by the particular treatment or colour represented.

    Photographic colour processes

    • Technicolor
    • Kinemacolor
    • Anscocolor
    • Ferraniacolor
    • Fujicolor
    • Kodachrome
    • Eastmancolor

    For digital colour encoding, colour space, and transfer function, see Colour in the Item chapter. Record these characteristics at Manifestation level only where they apply to the associated Items.

    Manifestation-level information should describe characteristics applicable to the associated Items; record Item-specific characteristics and differences in the Item descriptions. These groupings do not require separate database fields.

    8.3.5 Extent of a Manifestation1¶

    For a Manifestation describing a particular release or edition, record its expected or “ideal” extent as a reference for assessing the completeness of associated Items, using appropriate authoritative sources where feasible. Where a Manifestation groups varied production materials or derivatives, a single expected extent need not apply to every Item. Record the actual extent of each object at Item level.

    8.3.5.1 Logical Extent of a Manifestation¶

    Logical extent is the number of discrete units that make up the Manifestation, for example reels, discs, or digital components. State the type of unit being counted.

    For digital Manifestations, distinguish the number of logical components from the number of constituent files, stating what is being counted. Physical storage units, such as LTO tapes holding copies, do not determine the logical extent of the digital Manifestation.

    Example

    A home video publication: DVD in 2 discs

    A theatrical print: in 6 reels

    A digital publication comprising 3 files

    Record the number of the logical units of a Manifestation in Arabic numerals, and, if necessary, specify the type of unit. Selection should be made from a controlled list of terms, e.g:

    • Reel
    • Roll
    • Cassette
    • Cartridge
    • Loop
    • Disc
    • File

    If the number of the logical units of a Manifestation is uncertain, use a question mark following the unit count12 or record the uncertain number preceded by “approximately.”13

    If the number of the logical units of a Manifestation is indeterminate, record the information using a value of “unknown.”

    Alternatively, provide for a distinguishing “precision” field specifying if the unit number is exact, approximate or unknown.

    8.3.5.2 Physical Extent of a Manifestation2¶

    Physical extent is the total “ideal” length or footage of the medium carrying the moving image Manifestation (using appropriate authoritative secondary sources where feasible such as filmographies, censorship visas, etc.).

    The actual length is a characteristic of a singular Item since it can be different for multiple Items exemplifying the same Manifestation.

    Record the total length in Arabic numerals, in metres or feet.

    For digital Manifestations, record the total file size where it is a defined characteristic of the Manifestation. Otherwise, record file sizes at Item level. Use Arabic numerals followed by MB, GB, or TB as appropriate.

    The Unit of Measurement (feet, metres, GB, etc.) can be provided in a separate field. This could be two separate fields side by side – one for numbers and one for size measurement.

    If the length of a Manifestation is uncertain, use a question mark following the unit count or record the uncertain number preceded by “approximately.” In a note, give an explanation for the estimated footage or metre count, where known.

    If the length of a Manifestation is unknown, record the information using a value of “unknown.”

    Alternatively, provide for a distinguishing “precision” field specifying if the length is exact, approximate or unknown.

    8.3.5.2.1 Physical Extent of an Aggregate Manifestation¶

    Record the extent of the Aggregate Manifestation as a whole where known, for example as stated on the container, using the guidance above for the relevant medium. Where useful, also record the extent of individual constituents, making clear what each measurement describes—for example, a constituent film or a reel. Applicable constituent measurements already available through linked descriptions need not be recorded again. This information may be recorded using the available descriptive elements or notes.

    For more information about aggregates, see Aggregates (Compilations, Multi-component productions).

    8.3.5.3 Duration of a Manifestation3¶

    Manifestation duration represents the expected or “ideal” total running time under specified playback conditions, for example a specific frame rate. Sources may include information on containers, accompanying material, metadata associated with a digital object, or authoritative secondary sources such as filmographies and censorship records. Where a reported duration is known to be incorrect, record the accurate duration and explain the discrepancy in a note.

    The actual duration is usually considered a characteristic of a singular moving image Item, since it can differ among multiple Items exemplifying the same Manifestation.

    Use a duration notation appropriate to the cataloguing system and institutional practice. Examples include minutes, hours/minutes/seconds expressed as HH:MM:SS, or ISO 8601 duration notation. For example, 90 minutes may be expressed as 90 min, 01:30:00, or PT1H30M. Record only the precision supported by the available information. Duration can also support estimates of digital storage requirements for digitisation projects.

    If the duration/running time of a Manifestation is uncertain, use a question mark following the unit count or, if the system allows, use the qualifier “Duration Precision” and add the term “approximate.” (see Duration Precision)

    In a note, give an explanation for the estimated duration/running time, if such information applies.14

    If the duration/running time of a Manifestation is unknown, record the information using a value of “unknown.”

    8.3.5.3.1 Duration of an Aggregate Manifestation¶

    Record the duration of the Aggregate Manifestation as a whole where known, for example as stated on the container. Make clear whether it describes a continuous presentation, an individual programme or version, or the combined running times of several programmes or versions. Do not add the durations of components that play simultaneously.

    Where useful, record the running times of individual constituents using the available descriptive elements or a note.

    Applicable constituent measurements already available through linked descriptions need not be recorded again.

    Example

    DVD “The Audrey Hepburn Collection” contains three films with additional features. Running times for the three units are given on the container.

    These running times may be added to give a combined total. Make clear whether the timings cover the films alone or include additional features, and optionally record the individual running times.

    For more information about Aggregates see Aggregates (Compilations, Multi-component productions).

    8.3.5.3.2 Duration of a Manifestation associated with a Work/Variant of the Silent Era¶

    When recording duration of Manifestations of a Work/Variant of the silent era, take into account that the rate of frames per second varied over the years and between Variants/Manifestations. Also take into account the so-called “stretch frame” practice, adopted after the silent era, which “increases the number of frames printed on a film to enable films shot at silent speeds to be projected at sound speed and retain the original temporal characteristics.” 15

    Indicate the frame rate on which the duration is based. Where a film print or digital object uses repeated frames to preserve the running time of a slower-speed source, explain this in a note and distinguish the playback rate from the source film’s projection rate.16 See also Frame Rate.

    8.3.5.3.3 Duration of a Broadcast Manifestation associated with a Work/Variant¶

    When recording duration for Broadcast Manifestations there are two potential sets of data: a Slot Duration and an Actual Running Time. If the system allows, note the type of duration in the Duration Type element (see Duration Type).

    Slot Duration is the scheduled length of the broadcast slot, which may include advertisements or other material outside the programme. Sources may include television listings or schedules. Actual Running Time is the duration of the programme as broadcast, excluding those insertions. For example, an episode of “Coronation Street” may have a Slot Duration of 30 minutes but an Actual Running Time of 24 minutes.

    An Item recording may include advertisements or surrounding broadcast material, or may be incomplete. Record its actual duration as described under Item Duration.

    Record the start and end times of the broadcast where available, preferably using 24-hour clock notation, for example 23:00–23:30. Indicate whether the times are scheduled or actual, and whether they describe the programme or the whole slot. Do not calculate the programme’s running time from start and end times that include advertisement breaks: a broadcast from 23:00 to 23:30 may contain only 24 minutes of programme.

    It is for an institution to choose whether to record both sets of data in each instance or limit to either just slot or actual broadcast/running times.

    8.3.5.3.4 Duration Precision¶

    In this qualifier, note whether the duration is exact, approximate, or estimated. “Stock maximum” describes a tape’s labelled maximum playing time rather than duration precision. For its optional use at Item level, see Item Duration.

    8.3.5.3.5 Duration Type¶

    Where appropriate, add the type of duration being described. For broadcast materials, examples include “Slot Duration” and “Actual Running Time.”

    8.3.6 Notes for Manifestations¶

    Notes for Manifestations are annotations providing additional information relating specifically to Manifestation attributes and relationships.17 See Cataloguer’s Notes.

    8.3.7 Date and Country of Manifestation¶

    Dates and country of Manifestation are not elements of a Manifestation under EN 15907, as in the latter it envisages this data being on linked Publication Events. However, in some systems they may be structured as elements of a Manifestation. For further consideration of this see Boundaries between Manifestations and Events
