


!!! abstract "Definition" 
A moving image restoration is "the process of compensating for loss, damage and degradation by returning a work of art, an image or artefact to close to it's original content"[^1]. 

It is the complex process of returning a moving image to a known, earlier state that is as faithful as possible to its original release, respecting its authenticity, historical integrity, and the creators' intentions. It involves research, technical repair of physical damage, and the removal of time-based deterioration using both analogue and digital technologies.

Where the original release may have been heavily cut and censored, or there were multiple released versions, then this may involve re-inserting footage that did not exist in the original release, but was intended by the creator.

For a comprehensive discussion around the ethics of restoration, see the [FIAF Code of Ethics](https://www.fiafnet.org/pages/Community/fiaf-code-of-ethics-revised-2025-en.html#:~:text=1.6%20FIAF%20affiliates%20acknowledge%20the,one%20of%20its%20original%20formats) and further publications created by the [FIAF Technical Commission](https://www.fiafnet.org/pages/E-Resources/Technical-Commission-Resources.html).

Assess whether a restoration presents an existing version or introduces changes that distinguish another version or Work, following the guidance on [Boundaries between Works and Variants](../boundaries/boundaries_between_works_and_variants/index.md). Restoration alone does not determine whether a new Work or Variant record is needed.

Cataloguing for Restoration projects usually involves two core aspects:

•	The creation of new records; particularly Variants, Manifestations, and Items, and occasionally Works 

•	The enhancing and updating of existing records to improve data

As existing Items are inspected and assessed as part of the materials selection process in a restoration, it gives the ideal opportunity for identification, or to fill in any gaps in data or information relating to those existing Item records. 

Similarly, extra research carried out as part of a restoration project may provide further relevant information about both the original Work and restoration(s), e.g. missing credits, production details, etc. Add newly established information to the relevant descriptions, identifying its sources and any uncertainty.

Distinguish newly discovered information about original contributors from responsibility for restoration and changes to the moving-image content. Restoration contributors may be associated with the relevant [Manifestation](../agents/agents_for_manifestations/index.md) or [Preservation/Restoration Event](../events/further_event_types/index.md#sec-preservation_restoration), according to the scope of their contribution. Additional credits do not, by themselves, require a new Work or Variant; assess changes to content under the boundary guidance.

Restorations are linked to [Events](../events/further_event_types/index.md#sec-preservation_restoration) and [Agents](../agents/index.md).

## Restorations - Works/Variants

Some restorations are simply improving the film/colour/sound quality of deteriorating original prints, without inserting any missing or extra scenes or new music scores, etc.

Such improvements do not, by themselves, require a new Work or Variant record. Record the technical characteristics of the resulting Items and document the restoration activities through Events or equivalent information where Events are not supported.

Restoration should normally be described within the existing Work. A distinct version may warrant a Variant, or be distinguished through Manifestations where Variants are not used. Treat the creation of a new Work for a restoration output as exceptional, and explain which Work boundary the result crosses.

In systems with fewer descriptive levels, restoration materials may be described together in a record titled, for example, “Film title [Restoration Production Materials]”, with a reference or relationship to the film’s description. Such a record groups materials associated with the restoration project; its creation does not, by itself, mean that the restored film constitutes a new Work. Identify the individual materials and their relationships as the system permits. See also [Titles and structuring for production materials](../titles/production_materials/index.md).

Distinguish reconstructing an existing version from creating a new editorial realisation. Restoring newly found scenes or an earlier ending may substantially change the version previously available without establishing a new Work. Document the evidence for the reconstruction and any uncertainty. A new editorial realisation may warrant a Variant; it does not automatically constitute a new Work. Apply the boundary guidance when assessing the result, rather than deciding from the extent of the differences alone.

Creating a previously missing Work record describes an existing Work; it does not mean that the restoration has created another Work. Likewise, the absence of a Variant level or suitable descriptive fields does not, by itself, establish a new Work, nor do additional credits alone.

Further information regarding whether a new Work is needed or not can be found in the [Boundaries between Works](../boundaries/boundaries_between_works/index.md) and [Boundaries between Works and Variants](../boundaries/boundaries_between_works_and_variants/index.md) sections of this Manual.

The original Work and any new restoration Work should be linked in an associative relationship with each other. Manifestations and Items embodying the new Work should be associated with it.

## Restorations - Manifestations

The Restoration Manifestation Type represents the outcome(s) of restoration events/activities, usually involving selection and aggregation of materials from diverse source elements to replicate an “original” or “ideal” manifestation.

A Restoration Manifestation may be used to group outputs of a restoration project. Determine the appropriate Manifestation associations using the [boundary guidance](../boundaries/boundaries_between_manifestations/index.md) and a consistent institutional policy. In-house derivatives, including outputs made to improve image, colour or sound quality, do not necessarily require a separate Manifestation. Describe the actual characteristics and provenance of each Item; association with one Manifestation does not imply identical content, extent, function or format.

Distinguish the dates of restoration activities from the original film’s dates and the publication or release dates of restored versions. For example, a restoration undertaken in 2024–2025 of a film originally released in 1921 has restoration activity dates of 2024–2025. Record any subsequent release date separately. Use the relevant [Preservation/Restoration](../events/further_event_types/index.md#sec-preservation_restoration) and [Publication Events](../events/further_event_types/index.md#sec-publication), or equivalent information where Events are not supported, to make clear what each date describes.

## Restorations - Items

Where a Restoration Manifestation is used, Items created by an archive as part of the restoration project may be associated with it. If the full released restored version of the moving image is also subsequently acquired, e.g. a published DVD/Blu Ray, then describe that acquisition through the relevant Home Viewing Manifestation, using an existing Manifestation record where applicable, and create a new Item record for the acquisition.

Events, such as the premiere of a restored film, would be linked with a new Theatrical Manifestation for the release of the restoration, rather than with the Restoration Manifestation. Again, if a full final print of the restored film exists then it should be created as a new Item and linked to a Theatrical Manifestation. [Need to check scenarios for this as depends on context - as mentioned below]

Under the Restoration Manifestation approach, Item elements created as restoration outputs in the production of the restored film may be linked under that Manifestation, e.g. separate Magenta, Yellow and Cyan Separation Negative Items, soundtracks, various footages, intertitle reels, etc.

Each copy made from a pre-existing Item for a restoration project should have a new Item record where it is retained and managed as a distinct object. Determine its Manifestation association according to the approach described above.

All the existing Items from which the copies have been made (ie. the source Items) should remain with their existing Manifestations and not be moved to link or associate with the Restoration Manifestation.

Record the source Item or Items used to produce each restoration output, using persistent identifiers or other reliable references where available. Where an output combines material from several sources, describe what each source contributed so the output’s provenance can be understood. Identify reconstructed additions and any uncertainty or gaps in the source information.

Detailed information about the individual stages of the work — including what was done, when, by whom, using which equipment and for what reason, as well as the source and resulting Items or Manifestations — can be documented and linked through appropriate Preservation/Restoration Events, or in relevant fields within an Item where a system does not have Events.

Where to link any final complete print Item record depends on context. 

If the institution is also going to be the main distributor and the final print is for this purpose, then it too can also be linked under this same Restoration Manifestation. It represents the final outcome of the restoration project and the institution itself is the creator and provider of this print rather than simply acquiring a print of the Theatrical release.

However, if an institution was involved with a restoration project in conjunction with other institutions and acquires a copy of the final print released theatrically for its collection, then any Item for that would be linked to a Theatrical Manifestation for the moving image.

Digital file Items of Restorations that are used for internet streaming by the institution itself should be linked to a new Internet Manifestation.

The same data can be collected, grouped and recorded in any system. For example: 

If an index card catalogue, or non-relational database, is used then add the information to an existing record for the original film, possibly under a heading of “Restoration version + [date]”. The original source of any new Items for the project should also be recorded. Or else create a new record for the restoration version, and add the details on that, relating it to the original film record using a “See also” reference
